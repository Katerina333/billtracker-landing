A multi currency budget app has to deal with a simple fact: you earn in one currency, but you don't always pay in it. Bill Tracker handles that in two ways. Every space — home, a trip, your parents' household abroad — has its own currency. And with Premium you can log an expense in a different currency, exactly as you paid it, without the app guessing an exchange rate for you.

## When one currency isn't enough

More households live across currencies than you might think:

- **You live abroad and pay for things back home.** Rent in euros, but a phone contract, an insurance or a family bill in your home currency.
- **You live near a border.** A tank of fuel or the weekly shop on the other side, paid in a different currency every few weeks.
- **You buy online in dollars.** Subscriptions, apps and orders priced in a foreign currency show up as small, scattered amounts.
- **You travel.** A trip has its own currency, and a day trip across another border adds a third one.

A budget app that knows only one currency forces you to convert each of these in your head, on the spot, with whatever rate you happen to remember. That's how small mistakes pile up.

## How Bill Tracker works as a multi currency budget app

- **A currency per space.** A space has its own members, envelopes, budget and currency. Your home space can be in dollars and a second space for the flat abroad in euros — switch between them in one tap.
- **Expenses in other currencies (Premium).** Paid in another currency? Log it in that currency. The amount is kept exactly as typed, with no conversion, and listed separately from the budget totals.
- **No exchange rates fetched, ever.** Bill Tracker doesn't convert currencies or pull rates from the internet. What you see is what you entered — no surprises from a rate you didn't choose.
- **Fast logging, even offline.** Amount, envelope, save, on a large numpad. Logging works without a connection, and Siri can log an expense too: "Log expense in Bill Tracker".
- **No bank connection.** The app never reads your accounts. You type each expense, and there's no account to create.

## What a month looks like across two currencies

A couple living in the US, with a home space in dollars. In October they also paid a few things in euros during a family visit and one online order in pounds. Here are the home envelopes at the end of the month:

| Envelope | Budget | Spent | Left |
|---|---|---|---|
| Groceries | 700 | 642.30 | 57.70 |
| Transport | 250 | 188.00 | 62.00 |
| Eating out | 200 | 194.50 | 5.50 |
| Household | 150 | 96.20 | 53.80 |
| **Total** | **1,300** | **1,121.00** | **179.00** |

Below the totals, listed on their own, are the entries in other currencies: €46.80 for a dinner, €22.00 for train tickets and £18.99 for an order. They're kept exactly as typed. If you'd rather count them inside your dollar envelopes, there's a simple manual habit: once the card statement shows the amount actually charged in dollars, log that figure in the home currency as well — then your totals reflect the rate your bank really used. The foreign-currency entry stays outside the totals, so nothing is counted twice.

## One space per currency for longer stays

If part of your life runs in another currency for months — a flat you rent out abroad, a semester in another country, a parent's household you help with — give it its own space in that currency. Each space has separate envelopes and its own budget, so the two never mix, and insights show income vs expenses and top categories for each one. Unlimited spaces are part of Premium.

## FAQ

### Is there a budget app for multiple currencies?

Yes. In Bill Tracker each space has its own currency, and with Premium you can log an expense in a different currency. It's kept exactly as you typed it and listed separately from the budget totals.

### Does Bill Tracker convert currencies automatically?

No. Bill Tracker doesn't convert currencies or fetch exchange rates. An expense in another currency stays in that currency, so the amount always matches what you paid.

### Can I have budgets in different currencies?

Yes. Each space has its own currency, envelopes and budget — for example one in dollars for home and one in euros for a flat abroad. More than one space needs Premium.
