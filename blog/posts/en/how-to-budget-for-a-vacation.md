To budget for a vacation, decide the total before you book anything, split it into the costs you pay up front and the money you'll spend on the trip, and turn the on-trip part into a daily amount. Then log what you spend while you're away and compare it with that daily amount each evening. That's the whole method — the steps below show how to do each part with real numbers.

Most vacation budgets don't fail at the booking stage. They fail on day three, when the ice creams, taxis and "we're on holiday" dinners add up faster than anyone noticed.

## Step 1: Set the vacation budget before you book

Start from what your household can actually put towards the trip, not from the hotel you'd like. Two numbers help:

- **What you can set aside each month** without squeezing groceries or bills.
- **How many months there are until the trip.**

Multiply them and you have a realistic ceiling. If you can set aside 445 a month and the trip is ten months away, the vacation budget is 4,450 — and that's the number every booking has to fit inside.

If you move the money to a separate account each month, you can log the transfer as a recurring monthly expense in a "Vacation fund" envelope, so your home budget shows it every month. The running total of the fund you keep yourself — a note on your phone is enough.

## Step 2: Split the vacation budget into categories

A single total is too vague to steer by. Split it into the costs you'll pay before you leave and the money you'll spend once you're there. Here's a family of four, seven nights by the sea:

| Vacation budget | Amount |
|---|---|
| Flights | 1,200 |
| Accommodation (7 nights) | 1,400 |
| Travel insurance | 120 |
| Getting to and from the airport | 80 |
| Food & drinks | 840 |
| Local transport | 140 |
| Activities & tickets | 350 |
| Souvenirs & extras | 105 |
| Buffer | 215 |
| **Total** | **4,450** |

The first four rows — 2,800 — are paid before you go. The last five — 1,650 — are the money you'll spend on the trip, and that's where budgets usually slip.

The buffer is not optional. Something always comes up: a forgotten charger, a rainy day spent at a museum, a pharmacy visit. A buffer of a few percent keeps one surprise from breaking the plan.

## Step 3: Turn the trip money into a daily amount

"1,435 for spending" is hard to feel on a Wednesday afternoon. "205 a day" isn't. Divide each on-trip envelope by the number of days:

| Envelope | Trip budget | Per day |
|---|---|---|
| Food & drinks | 840 | 120 |
| Local transport | 140 | 20 |
| Activities & tickets | 350 | 50 |
| Souvenirs & extras | 105 | 15 |
| **Total** | **1,435** | **205** |

Keep the buffer out of the daily number — it's for surprises, not for an extra dinner. If a day ends under its amount, the difference simply stays in the envelope for a bigger day later, like the boat trip you've been looking forward to.

## How to budget for a family vacation: what people forget

Family trips have costs that don't appear on the booking confirmation. Go through this list before you fix the numbers:

- **Getting to the airport or station** — parking, a taxi at 5 a.m., a train ticket.
- **Food on travel days** — airport and motorway prices are not grocery prices.
- **Kids' extras** — the pool inflatable, the second sun hat, the souvenir everyone wants on the last day.
- **Tourist taxes and deposits** — some places charge them on arrival.
- **Things you buy for the trip** — sunscreen, swimwear, an adapter.
- **The week after** — the fridge is empty when you get home, and the first shop is often bigger than usual.

Add whatever applies to your trip to the right envelope now, while the budget is still on paper.

## Step 4: Track your vacation spending as it happens

The plan only works if you know where you stand on day four, not two weeks after you're home. That means logging every purchase when it happens, by whoever paid — card, cash, it doesn't matter.

- **Log right after paying.** Ten seconds at the till beats an hour of guessing later.
- **Everyone who pays, logs.** A trip budget only one person updates is always behind.
- **Check the envelopes each evening.** Compare what's left with the days you still have.

When you're home, look at the trip space once more:

2. **Which one was too generous?** Move the difference to where you needed it.

## FAQ

### How much should I budget per day on vacation?

There's no single right amount — the destination, how you eat and what you plan to do change it completely. Decide the total on-trip money first, subtract a buffer, and divide the rest by the number of days. That daily figure fits your trip far better than an average from somewhere else.

### Should flights and accommodation be part of the vacation budget?

Yes. Put everything the trip costs into one total, including what you pay months ahead. Then split it into costs paid before you leave and money spent on the trip, so you can see how much is really left once you arrive.

### How do I budget for a vacation abroad in another currency?

Set the on-trip envelopes in the currency you'll pay in, so the numbers you see match the prices around you. ### What if we overspend in the first few days?

Recalculate the daily amount for the days that are left, and decide together which envelope gives way — usually souvenirs or activities rather than food. Use the buffer only for real surprises, not to cover a planned treat.
